# Federalist No. 10
## James Madison (1787)
### Taking Back Agency: Digital Humanities for Civic Educators

A non-linear inquiry into controlling the violence of faction in a constitutional republic.

---

## 1. What is a Faction?

> {#faction-def}
> "By a faction, I understand a number of citizens, whether amounting to a majority or a minority of the whole, who are united and actuated by some common impulse of passion, or of interest, adverse to the rights of other citizens, or to the permanent and aggregate interests of the community."

*Key Takeaway:* Factions are not simply political parties; they are groups whose interests conflict with the public good or the rights of others.

---

## 2. Two Methods of Curing the Mischiefs of Faction

### Method A: Removing the Causes
- **Destroy the liberty essential to its existence**
  - *Madison's verdict:* Worse than the disease. Liberty is to faction what air is to fire.
- **Give every citizen the same opinions, passions, and interests**
  - *Madison's verdict:* Impracticable. As long as the reason of man continues fallible, differing opinions will form.

---

### Method B: Controlling the Effects
- **Minority Faction:**
  - Relieved by the republican principle: regular vote defeats it at the ballot box.
- **Majority Faction:**
  - The true danger to popular government.
  - *Madison's solution:* The **Extended Republic** (a large sphere of diverse interests prevents a majority from discovering and acting upon a common tyrannical impulse).

---

## 3. Deliberation & Historiographical Audit

### The Missing Voices
- Whose perspective is absent from Madison's calculation?
  1. Enslaved populations and disenfranchised laborers whose interests were omitted from the constitutional compromise.
  2. Anti-Federalist dissenters (e.g., *Brutus*), who argued that a large republic would alienate representatives from their constituents and foster distant oligarchy.

---

## 4. Classroom Inquiries (DBQ)

1. Is Madison's confidence in the "extended sphere" justified in the age of algorithmic social media, where geographic dispersion no longer slows factional coordination?
2. How does Madison define "the public good," and who had the institutional power to define it in 1787 versus today?
